# Justice League of Greater Lansing Michigan: Design Discovery

## Discovery status

This artifact is based on verified information already available from the existing organization website, prior project work, and stakeholder communications. Unverified items are marked explicitly and must not be treated as facts during implementation.

## Organization

- **Name:** Justice League of Greater Lansing Michigan
- **Organization type:** Nonprofit, community advocacy, reparations education, scholarship, membership, fundraising, and event organization
- **Primary geography:** Greater Lansing, Michigan
- **Current website:** https://www.justiceleagueglm.org/
- **Current platform:** Wix
- **Known stakeholders:** Prince Jerold Solace, President & Co-Founder; additional administrative and committee stakeholders exist
- **Known recurring activity:** Monthly organizational meetings, newsletters, scholarship activities, fundraising events, community education, and ad-booklet campaigns

## Business goals

1. Communicate the organization’s mission and legitimacy clearly.
2. Increase participation in events, meetings, campaigns, and community programs.
3. Support fundraising, donations, ticket sales, advertisements, and sponsorship opportunities.
4. Explain scholarship and reparations-related work with credible evidence and context.
5. Keep supporters informed through newsletters, event pages, and timely updates.
6. Make the site easier to maintain without weakening content hierarchy or accessibility.

## Conversion hierarchy

### Primary conversion

**Participate in the organization’s current priority action.** The exact action changes by campaign and may include event registration, donation, scholarship engagement, membership, advertisement purchase, or advocacy participation.

Because the primary action changes seasonally, the website should use a stable conversion architecture with a configurable campaign priority rather than hard-coding one permanent CTA across every page.

### Secondary conversions

- Subscribe to or read the newsletter
- Attend an event or monthly meeting
- Donate
- Become a member or supporter
- Submit or purchase an advertisement
- Learn about scholarships and reparations work
- Contact the organization

## Audiences

### Primary audiences

- Greater Lansing residents interested in racial justice and reparations
- Existing supporters and members
- Donors and event attendees
- Scholarship applicants, recipients, and families
- Community partners, churches, and local organizations

### Secondary audiences

- Sponsors and advertisers
- Media and public officials
- Grant makers and institutional partners
- Researchers, educators, and people seeking historical context
- Volunteers and prospective committee members

## Critical user questions

1. What is the Justice League of Greater Lansing Michigan?
2. What does the organization mean by reparations and how does it act locally?
3. What programs, scholarships, events, and campaigns are active now?
4. How can I attend, donate, join, advertise, volunteer, or otherwise participate?
5. Who leads the organization and how is it governed?
6. What evidence demonstrates community activity and impact?
7. Are event dates, prices, registration limits, and deadlines current?
8. How will donated or raised funds support the mission?

## Trust barriers

- The subject matter can be politically sensitive and requires careful, factual communication.
- Visitors may not understand the relationship between education, advocacy, scholarships, events, and fundraising.
- Outdated event, newsletter, ticket, or campaign information can reduce credibility.
- Generic nonprofit copy could make the organization appear interchangeable with unrelated charities.
- Poor hierarchy may bury active participation opportunities.
- Unclear leadership, governance, financial use, or program outcomes could weaken donor trust.
- Stock or synthetic imagery would be especially harmful if presented as real community participation.

## Brand position

The organization should feel:

- Community-rooted
- Historically aware
- Serious but welcoming
- Credible and transparent
- Action-oriented
- Proud, dignified, and locally specific
- Capable of handling institutional information without becoming bureaucratic

The organization should not feel:

- Generic corporate nonprofit
- Partisan campaign merchandise site
- Overly polished in a way that erases local authenticity
- Visually aggressive without educational context
- Dependent on trendy gradients, glass effects, or generic card grids

## Content priorities

1. Mission and local relevance
2. Current campaign or event
3. Scholarships and community outcomes
4. Reparations education and issue context
5. Ways to participate
6. Leadership, governance, and organizational history
7. Events, newsletters, and updates
8. Donation, sponsorship, and advertising opportunities
9. Contact information

## Known content and assets

- Registered Justice League logos using the ® symbol
- Existing event and newsletter content
- Scholarship-related communications and recipient information
- Event registration pages
- Organization leadership information
- Existing website photography and campaign assets
- Google Drive and newsletter resources used in operations

## Media direction

- Prioritize authentic event, scholarship, leadership, meeting, and community photography.
- Use captions to identify context, date, and relevance when known.
- Do not use AI-generated people as members, recipients, donors, or community participants.
- Historical imagery may be used only with rights, context, and readable captions.
- When authentic media is limited, use strong typography, documents, timelines, maps, quotations, and verified data instead of generic stock photos.

## Technical constraints

- Current site is managed through Wix.
- The design system should remain practical for staff updates and recurring campaigns.
- Event pages, registration, payments, newsletters, and forms may depend on Wix applications or integrations.
- Page performance may be affected by Wix scripts, embedded forms, video, and third-party widgets.
- The design must reach WCAG 2.2 Level AA and support deliberate mobile composition.

## SEO priorities

- Brand and organization-name searches
- Greater Lansing community and racial-justice relevance
- Reparations education and local advocacy content
- Scholarship program information
- Event and fundraiser discovery
- Organization, Event, Article, and Breadcrumb structured data where accurate

## Accessibility priorities

- Clear headings and reading order for educational content
- Keyboard-accessible navigation, forms, donation, and registration flows
- Visible focus and high contrast
- Accessible event information, dates, prices, and deadlines
- Alt text and captions for community and historical imagery
- Reduced-motion support
- Accessible PDFs or equivalent HTML for important documents

## Design implications

- The homepage should behave as an editorial and action hub, not a generic promotional landing page.
- The current priority campaign needs a visually dominant but replaceable module.
- Mission, history, programs, events, and participation paths require different content treatments rather than identical cards.
- Community proof should be documentary and specific.
- Mobile users should see current actions, event details, and contact pathways early.
- Long-form issue education needs readable measures, anchored navigation, citations, and related-action prompts.

## Assumptions requiring validation

- The organization intends to retain Wix for the next implementation phase.
- Donation processing and event registration will remain within current Wix-supported workflows.
- Existing authentic photography may be reused with appropriate permission.
- The organization wants the website to balance advocacy urgency with institutional credibility.
- The primary conversion will rotate by campaign rather than remain permanently fixed.

## Discovery conclusion

The brief is sufficient to generate art directions, but implementation should not begin until the organization confirms the campaign priority, content ownership, verified impact evidence, available photography, governance information, donation flow, and the intended scope of redesign.
