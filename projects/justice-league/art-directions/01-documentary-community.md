# Art Direction 01: Lansing in Action

## Taxonomy family

Documentary Community

## Strategic concept

Present the organization through real people, real places, and real civic participation. The website behaves like a living community record with a clear current-action layer.

## Business rationale

The Justice League’s credibility depends on demonstrating local presence, continuity, and human impact. Documentary structure allows scholarships, events, meetings, advocacy, and fundraising to feel connected rather than presented as unrelated programs.

## Target emotional response

“I understand who this organization serves, I can see that the work is real, and I know how to participate.”

## Visual-reference principles

- Documentary captions and location context
- Community journalism and oral-history presentation
- Event chronology and participant voices
- Clear civic calls to action

Do not copy the layout or visual identity of any single reference website.

## Color strategy

- Warm neutral reading canvas
- Deep, high-contrast text color
- One approved brand-derived action color
- Secondary colors sampled from authentic photography only after contrast validation
- Minimal gradients

## Typography strategy

- Humanist or editorial serif for selected headlines and quotations
- Highly readable sans serif for navigation, body copy, event details, and forms
- Moderate display scale rather than an oversized generic hero
- Captions and metadata receive a distinct but readable typographic role

## Layout strategy

### Homepage composition

1. Compact orientation header with mission and current priority action
2. Full-width documentary image or photo sequence with contextual caption
3. “What is happening now” editorial list, not three cards
4. Scholarship and community-impact story with real evidence
5. Reparations education introduction linked to deeper content
6. Event chronology or calendar strip
7. Ways to participate organized by commitment level
8. Leadership/governance and newsletter pathways

### Component grammar

- Square or lightly rounded media where the photograph needs containment
- Ruled editorial lists
- Captions attached to media
- Timeline and date markers
- Pull quotations with identified source
- Campaign action band that can be replaced seasonally
- Cards only for discrete events or documents when comparison is useful

## Image direction

- Candid community, scholarship, event, leadership, and meeting photography
- Wide environmental images mixed with intimate portraits and details
- Captions state verified event, person, date, or context
- Avoid generic symbolic stock photography
- Use image sequences to show participation and process

## Motion strategy

- Minimal image and section transitions
- Small state changes for filters, disclosure, registration, and donation interactions
- No parallax or constant scroll-trigger animation
- Full reduced-motion mode

## Mobile behavior

- Current priority action and next event appear early
- Photo sequences become vertically paced stories rather than tiny galleries
- Captions remain attached to images
- Event details use compact definition lists
- Navigation exposes Events, Scholarships, Get Involved, and Donate without deep nesting
- No sticky action should obscure educational content

## Accessibility considerations

- Captions and alt text must distinguish visual context from decorative imagery
- Avoid text embedded in event flyers; provide HTML equivalents
- Preserve reading order when editorial layouts collapse
- Historical and community photographs require meaningful context
- Ensure action color passes contrast in all states

## Performance considerations

- Documentary direction may become image-heavy
- Use responsive sources, defined dimensions, disciplined compression, and selective loading
- Do not use autoplay background video
- Limit initial homepage images and lazy-load later stories

## Risks

- Direction weakens if authentic photography is unavailable or poorly organized
- Too many stories can obscure the current campaign
- Emotional imagery cannot substitute for verified impact evidence
- Requires ongoing caption and asset discipline

## Industry fit

Excellent fit for a local community nonprofit, scholarship organization, and social-impact organization.

## Difference from alternatives

- More human and place-based than Editorial Evidence
- Less urgent and poster-like than Civic Momentum
- Uses photography and chronology as the primary organizing system

## Fit score

- Strategic fit: 9/10
- Content feasibility: 7/10 pending photography audit
- Accessibility risk: Moderate
- Performance risk: Moderate
- Distinctiveness potential: 9/10
