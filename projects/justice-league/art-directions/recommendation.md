# Art-Direction Recommendation

## Recommended direction

**Lansing in Action**, using selected editorial-evidence patterns from **The Case for Reparations**.

## Why this direction is recommended

The Justice League’s strongest differentiator is not a general nonprofit visual style. It is the organization’s specific connection to Greater Lansing, its scholarship work, its events, its leadership, and its ongoing community participation. A documentary community system gives those facts the highest credibility and makes the organization difficult to confuse with an unrelated nonprofit.

The direction also supports the organization’s recurring operational needs:

- Event and fundraiser promotion
- Scholarship storytelling
- Newsletters and organizational updates
- Community participation
- Leadership and governance
- Donation and advertisement campaigns

The recommendation is not a pure photography-first design. It should borrow the following from Editorial Evidence:

- Long-form reparations education templates
- Source notes and definitions
- Timelines and historical context
- Verified data and impact presentation
- Clear governance and transparency content

This hybrid remains one coherent direction because documentary community storytelling controls the overall composition, while editorial evidence is used only for content types that require explanation and proof.

## Why the alternatives are not the default

### The Case for Reparations

This direction offers the strongest educational and institutional framework, but it depends on a mature evidence library, disciplined long-form writing, verified metrics, and maintained source material. It may also understate the organization’s human and local character if implemented before that content is ready.

### Civic Momentum

This direction is highly effective for the Fall Celebration, advertisement campaigns, registration deadlines, and advocacy pushes. It is not recommended as the permanent site-wide system because recurring urgency can make evergreen scholarship, history, governance, and educational content feel secondary. Its strongest patterns should be retained as a campaign mode within the selected system.

## Proposed design-system model

Use a stable documentary-community foundation with two controlled content modes:

1. **Evidence mode** for reparations education, history, governance, impact, and scholarship reporting
2. **Campaign mode** for events, advertisements, fundraising deadlines, registration, and urgent participation

The modes must share typography, approved colors, navigation, spacing tokens, accessibility behavior, and component semantics. They should not look like separate websites.

## Recommended homepage concept

1. Compact mission orientation and current campaign action
2. Authentic Greater Lansing image or story sequence with caption
3. Current priorities presented as an editorial action list
4. Scholarship or community-impact feature
5. Concise reparations education entry point
6. Upcoming events and deadlines
7. Participation pathways by commitment level
8. Leadership, governance, newsletter, and contact pathways

## Conditions before approval

- Confirm the current primary conversion.
- Audit available photography and rights.
- Confirm approved logo, colors, and brand restrictions.
- Identify verified scholarship and impact evidence.
- Confirm redesign scope and Wix constraints.
- Determine who owns recurring campaign and event updates.
