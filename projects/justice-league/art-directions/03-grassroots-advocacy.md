# Art Direction 03: Civic Momentum

## Taxonomy family

Grassroots Advocacy

## Strategic concept

Frame the organization as a coordinated local movement with a clear issue, visible momentum, and immediate ways for supporters to participate.

## Business rationale

Events, fundraising campaigns, advertisement drives, meetings, and advocacy actions often require urgency. This direction gives recurring campaigns a strong visual framework while preserving space for education and legitimacy.

## Target emotional response

“This work is active now, people in my community are involved, and there is a clear action I can take today.”

## Visual-reference principles

- Civic posters and community campaign materials
- Bold typographic hierarchy
- Action calendars, progress markers, and participation pathways
- Print-inspired layouts rather than software-dashboard cards

Do not imitate a political campaign template or another advocacy organization’s identifiable composition.

## Color strategy

- High-contrast approved brand palette
- One dominant campaign color and one supporting accent
- Neutral reading sections for long-form education
- Large color fields used sparingly to create urgency
- No glassmorphism or ornamental gradients

## Typography strategy

- Bold condensed or grotesk display face for short action headlines
- Highly readable sans serif for body, forms, dates, and details
- Strong numeric treatment for dates, goals, deadlines, and event information
- Avoid all-caps body copy and overly compressed mobile headings

## Layout strategy

### Homepage composition

1. Campaign masthead with current action, deadline, and concise context
2. Immediate participation choices arranged by action type, not generic features
3. Local issue statement with concise evidence
4. Upcoming event or meeting schedule
5. Scholarship and community-impact proof
6. Movement timeline showing completed and current initiatives
7. Support options: donate, attend, advertise, volunteer, subscribe
8. Governance and educational resources

### Component grammar

- Poster-like action bands
- Hard-edged sections, rules, labels, and numbered steps
- Date blocks and campaign progress markers
- Large typographic statements balanced by calm reading surfaces
- Action lists rather than repeated rounded cards
- Campaign modules designed for replacement without redesigning the page

## Image direction

- Event crowds, speakers, scholarship moments, meetings, printed materials, signs, and community locations
- High-contrast crops and selective monochrome treatment may be used when accessible
- Avoid generic protest imagery unrelated to actual organization activity
- Do not use synthetic crowds or participants

## Motion strategy

- Short directional transitions for campaign changes, deadlines, and action feedback
- Optional restrained kinetic type for a single campaign statement
- No essential information conveyed only through motion
- Reduced-motion version removes all kinetic treatment

## Mobile behavior

- Current action, deadline, event date, and registration path appear within the first content sequence
- Large campaign type scales down aggressively to avoid one-word lines
- Participation actions become a prioritized vertical list
- Campaign progress and timelines use simple, readable mobile patterns
- Sticky donation or registration controls may be used only when they do not obscure content

## Accessibility considerations

- Bold color fields require rigorous text and focus contrast
- Condensed display type must not be used for long text
- Deadline and urgency must not rely on color alone
- Motion and animated text require reduced-motion alternatives
- Action language must be concrete and distinguishable out of context

## Performance considerations

- Generally moderate image requirements
- Avoid animation libraries for simple transitions
- Campaign background media must not become an oversized LCP asset
- Keep third-party forms and event widgets from blocking the initial action content

## Risks

- Could be interpreted as partisan or confrontational if messaging is not carefully balanced
- Urgency may overwhelm scholarship, history, and governance content
- Requires frequent campaign maintenance to avoid stale deadlines
- Bold typography can become generic if the actual organization voice is weak

## Industry fit

Strong fit for fundraising campaigns, event promotion, advocacy participation, and grassroots mobilization. Moderate fit for evergreen educational and governance content.

## Difference from alternatives

- More action- and deadline-led than Lansing in Action
- Less publication-like than The Case for Reparations
- Uses typography, campaign structure, and participation momentum as the primary organizing system

## Fit score

- Strategic fit: 8/10
- Content feasibility: 8/10
- Accessibility risk: Moderate to high
- Performance risk: Low to moderate
- Distinctiveness potential: 8/10
